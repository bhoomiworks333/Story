## 1. Final story concept

A small boy sits on a stool in the corner of his father's shop and swings his legs. He watches his father do
everything at once. He can't name the problem yet, but he can ask the question a child would ask:
**"Why does Papa have to remember everything?"**

That question stays with him. It's shown on screen as a single **red thread**. The thread comes out of the tangled
ball of chaos in his thought bubble and gets tucked into his notebook as a bookmark. It goes with him through
school, his first computer and years of learning. As an adult, he pulls the same thread out and uses it to connect
the disconnected parts of a business. The connected shape becomes AlfaStack.

**The red thread is the one visual device that carries the whole film.** It links the question, the years of
learning and the product, so we never need a "service list".

Core line the viewer should leave with: *People shouldn't have to carry the whole business in their heads.*

## 6. Camera and animation direction

- **Look:** a 2.5D paper diorama in layered cut-outs with parallax (foreground curtain/stool, midground characters,
  background shop wall), a subtle paper-grain overlay and a slight 12 fps "boil" jitter on the edges so it feels handmade.
- **Camera:** slow push-ins for emotion, whip-pans for comedy, one top-down move (notebook → brand), and one big pull-out (factory).
- **Transitions:** motivated only. The phone ring cuts to wide, the thought bubble becomes the ball, page flips carry the
  years, the thread leads to the brand, and a face match-dissolve closes the film. No generic wipes.
- **Timing:** comic beats are held for 6 to 8 frames before the punchline (the phone re-ringing, the critter sitting down).
- **Palette:** warm mustard, terracotta, cream and dusty pink for the childhood scenes, shifting to deep indigo and teal for the
  adult and tech scenes (as in your second reference). **The red thread is the only saturated red in the whole film.**
  **[NEEDS YOUR INPUT: AlfaStack brand colours and logo file (SVG/PNG)]**. I won't invent your logo.

## What I will actually build (production method)

I don't have an AI video generator in this environment. What I can build here is a **code-driven cut-out animation**:
every character and prop drawn as a layered vector, animated with keyframes, given paper texture, and rendered frame
by frame to an MP4 in headless Chromium with ffmpeg. I've confirmed that pipeline works here.

- **This is good at:** perfect character consistency (the same puppet at every age), exact timing, crisp subtitles,
  clean text and logo, easy revisions, and paper-cut texture.
- **This is weaker at:** the fluid, hand-drawn character acting in your reference. Expressions come from swapping
  eyes, brows and mouths, plus head tilts, blinks and squash and stretch. It will look like a well-made paper-puppet
  short, **not** a studio-animated film. If you need studio-level character acting, you need an animator, and this
  package is the brief you'd hand them.
- **Audio:** I'll deliver a silent master with burned-in subtitles plus a timing sheet. You add the VO (ideally
  Prashant) and a licensed track. I can add basic synthesised SFX placeholders.
